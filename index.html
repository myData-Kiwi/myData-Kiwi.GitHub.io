How it works

One box at home holds everything.

Your phone and laptop are windows onto it. They ask the box, and the box answers. The parts below are the plan, and none of them are built yet.

A home server

A small always-on computer, such as a Mac mini or a Linux box, stores your data and runs the AI model. Each app keeps its own database file there.

Your devices

A web app first, then phone and desktop apps. They reach the server over your home Wi-Fi, or through a private encrypted tunnel when you are away.

One assistant

myAssistant can use every app as a tool: search mail, find a photo, add a reminder. It reads freely, and it asks before it sends or deletes anything.

The apps

A family of apps over one store of data.

The first five come first because they hold what people ask an assistant about most. The rest follow one at a time.

Why a kiwi

A bird that never leaves the ground.

Most of our digital lives are kept in the cloud, on computers that belong to someone else. The kiwi cannot fly. It lives in a burrow it digs for itself, keeps to itself, and guards its own patch of ground.

That is what myData is meant to be: a home for your data that stays on the ground, on hardware you own.

  • Cannot flyYour data stays out of the cloud
  • Digs its own burrowRuns on your own home server
  • Shy and nocturnalPrivate by default
  • Finds food by smellSearch finds anything you have stored
  • Guards its territoryOnly your devices are let in
What it will not do

The limits, stated up front

  • Mail and calendar accounts still live with your provider. myData keeps a searchable copy at home, and the original stays where it is.
  • An iPhone does not let outside apps send iMessages, place calls hands-free, or listen for a custom wake word.
  • A model small enough to run at home makes more mistakes than a large cloud model. That is why sending and deleting always need your confirmation.
  • You run the server, so backups are your job. Encrypted offsite backup is part of the plan.
